Our current geographic hypothesis places Aviana's affiliation as **Névome (Pima Bajo)** at 50–65% probability based on San Javier's location in the Sonora River watershed, with **Yaqui** affiliation at 20–35% based on the documented mass deportations of 1902–1908 under Governor Izábal and the well-attested pattern of Yaqui families adopting mestizo identities to avoid forced removal. 23andMe testing of the present-day descendant confirms Indigenous American maternal ancestry (mitochondrial haplogroup B2) but is not tribe-specific. We approach the Pascua Yaqui Tribe with the respect appropriate to tribal sovereignty and with no presumption of affiliation; we seek consultation and guidance, not claim or recognition.

## Specific Questions and Requests

We respectfully request guidance on the following:

### 1. Oral History Collections

Does the Pascua Yaqui Tribe's Cultural Resources Department, or the ANARÓ oral history collection, hold any materials — interviews, recorded testimonies, community histories — that reference:

- Yaqui or Névome families in San Javier, Sonora (Sonora River watershed) during the period 1880–1940?
- Families displaced from the San Javier or Sahuaripa region during the Yaqui deportation campaigns (1902–1908)?
- The surname **Sagasta** (or variants: Zagasta, Sagaste) as a mission-origin indigenous surname in Sonora?
- Women who held land titles in central Sonora as nominal titleholders for non-indigenous operators during the Porfiriato or post-Revolutionary period?

### 2. Genealogical Guidance

We are currently conducting a search of **FamilySearch catalog 704681** (Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe parish, Sahuaripa, 1781–1961) for baptismal entries related to Aviana Sagasta and her children. We are specifically looking for indigenous parentage notations — *india, indígena, pima, de nación yaqui*, or similar caste markers. If the Cultural Resources staff has experience navigating these records for tribal-affiliation research, we would welcome methodological guidance.

### 3. Névome / Pima Bajo vs. Yaqui Determination

The primary geographic hypothesis places Aviana as Névome (Pima Bajo) based on San Javier's location in the Sonora River watershed. However, Yaqui presence in that region during periods of forced displacement cannot be excluded. Does the Tribe have knowledge of mixed Névome–Yaqui communities or inter-tribal family networks in the Sonora River region that might help resolve this determination?

### 4. Modern Institutional Pathway

The investigation's long-term goal — beyond the family's historical understanding — is to produce a documented account of how Porfirian mining law, the Mexican Civil Code's exclusion of concubines from property rights, power-of-attorney conventions, and Wells Fargo Express common-carrier rules combined to enable systematic extraction from an indigenous woman with no legal recourse. We believe this structural analysis may be of interest to the Tribe as a case study in the documented pattern of indigenous land dispossession in Sonora.

We are aware of the landmark **IACHR *Tribu Yaqui v. Mexico*** case (2023) and the historic recognition it represents. We do not presume to attach this family investigation to that proceeding without the Tribe's engagement and guidance. We raise it only to indicate that we understand the institutional framework and seek to work within it respectfully.

### 5. Referral

If the Cultural Resources Department is not the appropriate point of contact for this inquiry, we respectfully request a referral to the correct department, office, or community member.
