## Who We Are

My name is **Gabriel T. Arce Jr.** I am a descendant — fourth generation — of **María Manuela "Nela" Terminel Sagasta**, born August 7, 1910 in San Javier, Sonora, who lived to the age of 101 and died in Ontario, California in 2012. Nela was the daughter of Aviana Sagasta and Francisco L. Terminel, a Sonoran mining operator and later senator of the Mexican Republic (1932–1935).

Our family's oral history, transmitted continuously through four generations, holds that Aviana Sagasta was an indigenous woman who held legal title to significant land and water-rights properties in San Javier under arrangements made by Francisco Terminel — arrangements that, under the laws of the Porfiriato and post-Revolutionary Mexico, left her with no legal rights to the proceeds of those properties. The oral history further holds that Aviana traveled to a Wells Fargo Express office (most likely in Hermosillo or Nogales) in the mid-1930s seeking access to funds she believed were held in her name, and was denied.

**She was left with one cow.**

## What the Investigation Has Established

This investigation now spans a 95-claim relational database with documentary support from the following independently verified sources:

- **Servicio Geológico Mexicano (SGM) 2006** — confirms mining concessions at San Javier under the name **Luis Porchas**, consistent with the proxy-titleholder structure described in oral history.
- **INEHRM chronology** — confirms Francisco L. Terminel as one of five senators stripped of immunity (*desafuero*) on **December 14, 1935**, under President Cárdenas.
- **AGES Ramo Ejido-Dotaciones expediente 411.123/2174** — confirms that **453 hectares** were expropriated from Francisco Terminel and his brothers in Bacobampo, Etchojoa, Sonora in March 1938 — ejido territory that is today part of the traditional Yaqui homeland.
- **23andMe mtDNA results (Gabriel T. Arce Jr.)** — haplogroup **B2**, consistent with Uto-Aztecan maternal lineage (Névome Pima Bajo, Yaqui, Mayo). DNA alone cannot establish tribal affiliation; it is offered here as corroborating biological context only.
- **Geographic analysis** — San Javier (INEGI 260541) is located in the Sonora River watershed, which corresponds to the traditional habitat of the Névome (Pima Bajo) people, with a probability of **50–65%**. Yaqui presence in San Javier is assessed at **15–25%**, with geographic displacement during the deportation campaigns of 1902–1908 as a plausible explanatory factor.

The investigation has **permanently quarantined all AI-generated synthetic documents from prior research phases**, including fabricated transaction IDs, fictitious employee names, and a fraudulent restitution document (WF-RPA-2025-ASD-001). ## Specific Questions and Requests

We respectfully request guidance on the following:

### 1. Oral History Collections

Does the Pascua Yaqui Tribe's Cultural Resources Department, or the ANARÓ oral history collection, hold any materials — interviews, recorded testimonies, community histories — that reference:

- Yaqui or Névome families in San Javier, Sonora (Sonora River watershed) during the period 1880–1940?
- Families displaced from the San Javier or Sahuaripa region during the Yaqui deportation campaigns (1902–1908)?
- The surname **Sagasta** (or variants: Zagasta, Sagaste) as a mission-origin indigenous surname in Sonora?
- Women who held land titles in central Sonora as nominal titleholders for non-indigenous operators during the Porfiriato or post-Revolutionary period?

### 2. Genealogical Guidance

We are currently conducting a search of **FamilySearch catalog 704681** (Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe parish, Sahuaripa, 1781–1961) for baptismal entries related to Aviana Sagasta and her children. We are specifically looking for indigenous parentage notations — *india, indígena, pima, opata, india opata, de nación yaqui*, or similar caste markers. If the Cultural Resources staff has experience navigating these records for tribal-affiliation research, we would welcome methodological guidance.

### 3. Névome / Pima Bajo vs. Yaqui Determination

The primary geographic hypothesis places Aviana as Névome (Pima Bajo) based on San Javier's location in the Sonora River watershed. However, Yaqui presence in that region during periods of forced displacement cannot be excluded. Does the Tribe have knowledge of mixed Névome–Yaqui communities or inter-tribal family networks in the Sonora River region that might help resolve this determination?

### 4. Modern Institutional Pathway

The investigation's long-term goal — beyond the family's historical understanding — is to produce a documented account of how Porfirian mining law, the Mexican Civil Code's exclusion of concubines from property rights, power-of-attorney conventions, and Wells Fargo Express common-carrier rules combined to enable systematic extraction from an indigenous woman with no legal recourse. We believe this structural analysis may be of interest to the Tribe as a case study in the documented pattern of indigenous land dispossession in Sonora.

We are aware of the landmark **IACHR *Tribu Yaqui v. Mexico*** case (2023) and the historic recognition it represents. We do not presume to attach this family investigation to that proceeding without the Tribe's engagement and guidance. We raise it only to indicate that we understand the institutional framework and seek to work within it respectfully.

### 5. Referral

If the Cultural Resources Department is not the appropriate point of contact for this inquiry, we respectfully request a referral to the correct department, office, or community member.
