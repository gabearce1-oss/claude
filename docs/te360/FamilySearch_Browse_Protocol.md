# FamilySearch Browse Protocol — TSK-001 & TSK-002
## Catalogs 704679 (Civil) & 704681 (Parish) — Sahuaripa / San Javier, Sonora

## CRITICAL GEOGRAPHIC NOTE

- **Catalog 704679** = Registros civiles, municipio de Sahuaripa, Sonora, 1868–1994 (browsable with images directly online)
- **Catalog 704681** = Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe (Sahuaripa), 1781–1961 — covers the full colonial and republican period, 87 years before civil registration began

San Javier was historically administered under Sahuaripa municipality until State Law 109 (December 22, 1934) split off records. So San Javier records will appear inside the Sahuaripa municipal collection, not in a separate San Javier collection. This is why catalog 704679 is the correct target.

**Split-coverage caveat (1930–1934):** Per Claim GEO-003 (VERIFIED, confidence 9), for the 1930–1934 window records were split across THREE registries simultaneously — Hermosillo, Villa Pesqueira, and La Colorada — not just Sahuaripa. Nela's 1910 birth is safely inside the pre-split Sahuaripa window, but death-record searches for Francisco's siblings or Aviana that fall in 1930–1934 must check all three registries. Flag this in the search log.

## TRIBAL-NOTATION CONTEXT (read before browsing parish records)

Sahuaripa's first settlers were **Opata** — not Yaqui, not Névome Pima Bajo. Any baptism record with a tribal notation in Sahuaripa town proper would most likely read **opata** or **india opata**, not pima.

San Javier sits in the Centro Costa watershed, distinct from Sahuaripa proper — the **pima** notation is more plausible there. Catalog 704681 is the highest-priority target for caste/indigenous notations, which appear most reliably in the **1781–1860 period** (colonial-era baptism books used explicit caste vocabulary). After the Reform Laws of 1857–1859 secularized vital records, caste notations largely disappeared from civil records but persisted informally in parish books for another generation.

## INDEX QUALITY WARNING

The index is computer-aided (CAI) for parts of this collection — automated OCR transcribed handwritten 19th-century Spanish. Handwritten surnames like Terminel, Sagasta, and Aviana in 1880–1910 script will have been misread by the computer — they may appear as Terminol, Sagesto, Aviana, or simply not be indexed at all.

**Do not trust the index alone.** The only reliable method is manual image browse, film by film, for the years 1880–1920.

## ACCESS — TWO PATHS, USE BOTH

### Path 1 — Catalog Browse (for full microfilm images)

1. familysearch.org → Search → Catalog → type **704679** → click the camera icon next to each film roll
2. Repeat for **704681**

### Path 2 — Collection Browse (faster for targeted year ranges)

- **Civil:** familysearch.org → Search → Historical Records → "Mexico, Sonora, Civil Registration, 1861–1995" → Browse by Municipality → Sahuaripa → Births/Marriages/Deaths → year range
- **Parish:** "Mexico, Sonora, Catholic Church Records, 1657–1994" → Browse by Town → Sahuaripa → Parish → Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe

Path 2 is faster for targeted year ranges. Path 1 gives full microfilm access including pages not indexed.

## WHAT TO LOOK FOR — PARISH RECORDS (704681)

**THIS IS THE INDIGENOUS IDENTITY PIPE TARGET.** Look for baptism entries containing any of these Spanish-language notations:

- **india** — standard colonial/early republican notation for indigenous woman
- **indígena** — post-Reform era equivalent
- **opata** / **india opata** — most likely in Sahuaripa town proper
- **de nación yaqui** — explicit tribal identification
- **de nación mayo** — Mayo tribal identification
- **pima** — Pima/Névome notation (highest-probability match for Aviana Sagasta given San Javier geography)
- **mestiza** — mixed heritage
- Any caste notation: **castiza, lobo, coyote** — these appear in earlier records
- Godparent surnames: Porchas, Torres, López appearing alongside Terminel/Sagasta

## WHAT TO LOOK FOR — CIVIL RECORDS (704679)

- **Nacimientos:** Terminel as father OR mother's surname. Nela (María Manuela Terminel Sagasta) born ~August 7, 1910 = highest-value find in genealogical pipe.
- **Matrimonios:** Francisco Terminel + Aviana/Avina Sagasta
- **Defunciones:** Any Terminel or Sagasta death records
